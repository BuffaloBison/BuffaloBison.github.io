Purine Content in Foods and Beverages

High-Purine Foods (150-1000mg per 100g)

  • Organ Meats: Liver (360-400mg), kidney (210-250mg), sweetbreads (400-500mg)
  • Game Meats: Venison (210mg), rabbit (130mg)
  • Seafood: Anchovies (410mg), sardines (480mg), mackerel (280mg), herring (210mg)
  • Meat Extracts: Gravy, broth, consommé (variable, often 300-500mg)

Moderate-Purine Foods (50-150mg per 100g)

  • Meats: Beef (110-130mg), pork (120-150mg), lamb (120-140mg)
  • Poultry: Chicken (120-140mg), turkey (150mg)
  • Fish: Trout (170mg), tuna (160mg), salmon (170mg)
  • Legumes: Lentils (127mg), chickpeas (109mg), black beans (128mg)
  • Vegetables: Asparagus (23mg), mushrooms (58mg), spinach (57mg)

Low-Purine Foods (0-50mg per 100g)

  • Dairy: Milk (0-10mg), cheese (0-20mg), yogurt (0-10mg)
  • Eggs: Whole egg (2mg), egg white (3mg)
  • Grains: Rice (25mg), pasta (20mg), bread (20-40mg)
  • Fruits: Most fruits (10-30mg), especially cherries (7mg) and citrus fruits
  • Nuts/Seeds: Most nuts (10-40mg), with peanuts (79mg) being higher

Health Considerations

  • Gout Risk: High purine intake can increase uric acid levels, potentially leading to gout
  • Combined Risk: Foods high in both oxalates and purines (like spinach) may require special attention
  • Hydration: Important for both oxalate and uric acid management
  • Individual Tolerance: Responses to dietary purines vary between individuals

Food Preparation Tips to Reduce Oxalates and Purines

Reducing Oxalates:

  1. Boiling:
    • Boil high-oxalate vegetables (like spinach or beets) and discard the water
    • Can reduce oxalates by 30-90% depending on the food
    • Best for: Leafy greens, potatoes, root vegetables
  2. Soaking:
    • Soak beans, nuts, and grains before cooking
    • Use fresh water for cooking after soaking
    • Soak time: 4-12 hours (longer for legumes, shorter for nuts)
  3. Pairing with Calcium:
    • Combine high-oxalate foods with calcium-rich foods (dairy, calcium-fortified alternatives)
    • Calcium binds oxalates in the gut, reducing absorption
  4. Cooking Methods:
    • Steaming > Boiling > Roasting > Frying (in order of effectiveness at reducing oxalates)
    • Avoid using cooking water from high-oxalate foods in other dishes

Reducing Purines:

  1. Meat Preparation:
    • Boil meats and discard the broth (up to 50% purine reduction)
    • Avoid consuming meat drippings or gravies
    • Trim visible fat before cooking
  2. Seafood Tips:
    • Choose smaller, younger fish which typically have fewer purines
    • Avoid fish skin and dark meat areas
    • Opt for cooking methods that allow fat to drip away (grilling, broiling)
  3. Vegetable Management:
    • Most plant-based purines are less likely to affect uric acid levels
    • However, for those highly sensitive, blanching can help reduce purines

General Tips:

  • Hydration: Drink plenty of water to help flush out oxalates and uric acid
  • Portion Control: Smaller portions of high-oxalate/high-purine foods
  • Balanced Meals: Combine with low-oxalate, low-purine foods
  • Food Processing: Fermentation can reduce oxalates in some foods
  • Timing: Spread out high-oxalate/purine foods throughout the day
